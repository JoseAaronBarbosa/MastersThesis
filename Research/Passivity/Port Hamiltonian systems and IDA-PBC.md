## Port-controlled Hamiltonian (PCH) systems

Network modelling of energy-conserving lumped-parameter systems with independent storage elements leads to models of the form:[^1][^2]
$$\begin{align}
\dot{x}&=[J(x)-R(x)]\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(x)+g(x)u\\
y&=g^{T}(x)\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(x)
\end{align}$$
where $x$ are the energy variables, $H(x)$ is the total stored energy, $J(x)=-J^{T}(x)$ is the interconnection matrix (it only redistributes energy among the storage elements), $R(x)=R^{T}(x)\geq0$ is the dissipation matrix, and $u,y$ are conjugated port variables whose product is the power exchanged with the environment (forces and velocities in a mechanical system). The class strictly contains the Euler-Lagrange models.

The rate of change of the energy is:
$$\dot{H}=-\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}\right)^{T}R\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}+u^{T}y$$
Integrating gives the energy balance (supplied energy equals stored energy plus dissipated energy):
$$\int_{0}^{t}u^{T}(s)y(s)ds=H[x(t)]-H[x(0)]+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}\right)^{T}R\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}ds$$
If $H$ is bounded from below, the system is passive $u\to y$ with storage function $H$. With $u=0$ the energy is non-increasing, and damping injection $u=-K_{v}y$, $K_{v}>0$, increases the rate of decay.[^1]

### Manipulator as a PCH system

This form is not derived in the paper, which only uses $(q,p)$ coordinates in its concluding remarks. With $x=(q,p)$ and the generalized momentum $p=M(q)\dot{q}$:
$$H=\frac{1}{2}p^{T}M^{-1}(q)p+P(q),\qquad J=\begin{bmatrix}0&I\\-I&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad R=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\0&R_{f}\end{bmatrix},\qquad g=\begin{bmatrix}0\\I\end{bmatrix}$$
Friction is written as $F(\dot{q})=R_{f}(\dot{q})\dot{q}$ with $R_{f}\geq0$, and the natural output is $y=\partial H/\partial p=\dot{q}$. Using $\dot{p}=M\ddot{q}+\dot{M}\dot{q}$ with $\dot{M}=C+C^{T}$ (Christoffel construction of $C$) this recovers $M\ddot{q}+C\dot{q}+g+F=\tau$.[^2]

Correspondence with the structured learning module: $\hat{M}_{\theta}$ and $\hat{U}_{\theta}$ define an estimate $\hat{H}$, the positive semidefinite $\hat{D}_{\theta}$ plays the role of $\hat{R}$, and the skew-symmetric structure given by the Christoffel construction corresponds to the canonical $J$, which is known and does not need to be learned. In this reading the module learns the energy function and the dissipation of a PCH system.

## IDA-PBC design

The objective is to find a static feedback $u=\beta(x)$ such that the closed loop is a PCH system with a chosen structure and energy:
$$\dot{x}=[J_{d}(x)-R_{d}(x)]\frac{\partial H_{d}}{\partial x}(x)$$
where $H_{d}$ has a strict local minimum at the desired equilibrium $x^{*}$.[^1] The designer first fixes the desired interconnection and damping, $J_{d}=J+J_{a}$ and $R_{d}=R+R_{a}$, hence the name interconnection and damping assignment. Then the matching equation is solved for $\beta(x)$ and $K(x)=\partial H_{a}/\partial x$:
$$[J+J_{a}-(R+R_{a})]K=-[J_{a}-R_{a}]\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}+g\beta$$
Proposition 1 asks for:
- (i) Structure preservation: $J_{d}=-J_{d}^{T}$ and $R_{d}=R_{d}^{T}\geq0$.
- (ii) Integrability: $\partial K/\partial x$ is symmetric, so that $K$ is a gradient.
- (iii) Equilibrium assignment: $K(x^{*})=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(x^{*})$.
- (iv) Lyapunov stability: $\frac{\partial K}{\partial x}(x^{*})>-\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial x^{2}}(x^{*})$.

Under these conditions the closed loop is PCH with $H_{d}=H+H_{a}$. For systems of the form (1) with $g$ of full column rank, take a left annihilator $g^{\perp}$ ($g^{\perp}g=0$). The equation for $H_{a}$ is the linear PDE:
$$g^{\perp}[J+J_{a}-(R+R_{a})]\frac{\partial H_{a}}{\partial x}=-g^{\perp}[J_{a}-R_{a}]\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}$$
and the control is:
$$\beta=(g^{T}g)^{-1}g^{T}\left([J+J_{a}-(R+R_{a})]\frac{\partial H_{a}}{\partial x}+[J_{a}-R_{a}]\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}\right)$$
Unlike classical Lyapunov-based designs, the Lyapunov function is not guessed in advance: it comes out of the solution of the PDE.[^1]

### Stability proof (sketch)

The closed loop is PCH, so along trajectories:
$$\dot{H}_{d}=-\left(\frac{\partial H_{d}}{\partial x}\right)^{T}R_{d}\frac{\partial H_{d}}{\partial x}\leq0$$
Condition (iii) makes $x^{*}$ an extremum of $H_{d}$ and (iv) makes it an isolated minimum, so $H_{d}$ is a Lyapunov function and $x^{*}$ is stable. Asymptotic stability follows from LaSalle's invariance principle if the largest invariant set contained in $\{x:\nabla H_{d}^{T}R_{d}\nabla H_{d}=0\}$ is $\{x^{*}\}$, and the largest bounded level set $\{H_{d}\leq c\}$ estimates the domain of attraction.[^1][^3]

### Other results of the paper

- Universal stabilization (Proposition 2): every asymptotically stabilizing controller of a PCH system of form (1) can be generated by IDA-PBC. This is an existence result and does not say that the PDE is easy to solve.
- Energy balancing (Proposition 4): if $R_{a}=0$ and the natural damping satisfies $R\,\partial H_{a}/\partial x=0$, then $H_{d}$ equals the open-loop energy minus the energy supplied by the controller. For potential-energy shaping of a mechanical system this condition holds, because $H_{a}$ depends only on $q$ and $R$ acts only on the $p$ block.
- Passive output (Remark 4): IDA-PBC gives passivity with respect to $\tilde{y}=g^{T}\partial H_{d}/\partial x$, which coincides with the natural output $y$ if $g^{T}K\equiv0$.
- Kinetic energy modification (concluding remarks): choosing the interconnection matrix as
$$J_{d}(q,p)=\begin{bmatrix}0&M^{-1}(q)M_{d}(q)\\-M_{d}(q)M^{-1}(q)&J_{2}(q,p)\end{bmatrix}$$
modifies the inertia from $M(q)$ to a desired $M_{d}(q)$ ($J_{2}$ is given in terms of $M$, $M_{d}$ and $p$ in the paper). The paper only indicates this and refers to the work on underactuated mechanical systems for the details.[^4]
- Comparison with Euler-Lagrange PBC: designs that modify the kinetic energy by inverting the system along reference trajectories destroy the EL structure and impose a stable invertibility requirement on the plant.[^1][^5]
- Scope: the results are restricted to stabilization of fixed points. The tracking problem is left as future research.[^1]

## Relevance for the thesis

Idea under evaluation: use the nominal model $(\hat{J},\hat{R},\hat{H})$ to design the IDA-PBC feedback, learn the residual between the true plant and the nominal one, and handle the remaining bounded approximation error with a sliding mode action.

Why model knowledge is needed: the matching equation and the equilibrium condition involve $\partial H/\partial x$, $R$ and $g$ of the real plant. In particular, (iii) requires $\partial H/\partial x(x^{*})$ exactly, which for a manipulator at $x^{*}=(q^{*},0)$ is the true gravity vector $g(q^{*})$. With a nominal model the closed loop is the desired PCH system plus a perturbation, and $x^{*}$ is no longer an equilibrium of the real loop: there is a steady-state offset proportional to the residual.

Perturbed closed loop, under the assumption that the residual enters through the input channel (to be verified):
$$\dot{x}=[J_{d}-R_{d}]\frac{\partial H_{d}}{\partial x}+g(x)\left(\delta+w\right)$$
where $\delta$ is the residual from the model mismatch and $w$ is the additional control (learning plus robust action). Using $H_{d}$ as storage function:
$$\dot{H}_{d}=-\left(\frac{\partial H_{d}}{\partial x}\right)^{T}R_{d}\frac{\partial H_{d}}{\partial x}+\tilde{y}^{T}(\delta+w),\qquad\tilde{y}=g^{T}\frac{\partial H_{d}}{\partial x}$$
so $-\delta\to\tilde{y}$ is passive, and output strictly passive when the assigned damping dominates in the actuated channels (for example $R_{d}=\mathrm{diag}(0,K_{d})$ and $g=[0;I]$ give $\tilde{y}=\partial H_{d}/\partial p$ and a dissipation rate $\lambda_{min}(K_{d})$). This is the same structure as the passivity of $-\tilde{\Delta}\to s$ in the current design.

With the residual written as $\delta=W^{*}\Sigma_{\theta}+\varepsilon$ and the composite storage function $V=H_{d}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\{\tilde{W}^{T}K_{\omega}^{-1}\tilde{W}\}$, the same adaptation law cancels the trace term exactly, leaving $-\nabla H_{d}^{T}R_{d}\nabla H_{d}-\tilde{y}^{T}\varepsilon+\tilde{y}^{T}w$. A super-twisting action on $\tilde{y}$ would then be added as in the current composite Lyapunov function, with $H_{d}$ replacing $\frac{1}{2}s^{T}M(q)s$. The need for the Lipschitz certificate of the residual does not change.

Points to resolve:
- Regulation versus tracking: the paper stabilizes fixed points, while the thesis tracks $q_{d}(t)$ with $s=\dot{e}+\Lambda e$. The passive output $\tilde{y}=M_{d}^{-1}p$ is velocity-like, so a sliding variable that includes position error, as in the thesis, is still needed.
- Fully actuated robot: potential-energy shaping plus damping injection already stabilizes it (the current controller is of this type). The benefit of assigning a different inertia $M_{d}$ has to be justified, since the paper presents IDA-PBC mainly for underactuated systems and applications beyond mechanics.
- Matched uncertainty: the controller only has $\hat{p}=\hat{M}\dot{q}$, not the true momentum $p=M\dot{q}$. It has to be checked whether the mismatch in $M$ really enters only through $g$, which the sliding mode action requires.
